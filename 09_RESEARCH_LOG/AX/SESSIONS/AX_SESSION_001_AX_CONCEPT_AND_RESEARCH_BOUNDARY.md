## 4. Candidate Problem Definition

Can AX be defined by a structural change mechanism in which an AI capability removes or changes an existing organizational assumption and thereby causes redesign of Process, R&R, information flow, judgment flow, or control flow at the Operating Model level?

The proposed mechanism remains Candidate:

```text
AI Capability
→ machine-executable portion of an existing human function
→ changed necessity of an existing Process or R&R
→ Process or Role redesign
→ changed information, judgment, or control flow
→ Operating Model change
→ measurable organizational outcome
```

## 5. Working Boundary Claims

- `Agent deployment ≠ AX` unless the required structural change is demonstrated.
- `Automation ≠ necessarily AX` unless the required structural change is demonstrated.
- A productivity gain is an outcome observation, not by itself proof of AX.
- Judgment Architecture is one candidate pattern under AX, not the definition of AX.
- Technology requirements should be derived from validated organizational design requirements.

These are Working claims, not approved AX conclusions.

## 6. First Research Priority

Reverse-analyze historical ERP and DX cases before defining a detailed AX architecture.

For each case, extract at least:

1. technology capability;
2. pre-existing organizational assumption;
3. affected human function;
4. Process or R&R change;
5. information, judgment, or control-flow change;
6. Operating Model consequence;
7. measured outcome;
8. competing causal explanation;
9. non-transformative alternative or counterexample; and
10. confidence and evidence limitations.
